What are you costing, and on what basis?

The dollar-year, the location and the costing method decide what every number downstream means. Set them first and they will appear on the title block and on every export.

Identification

Cost basis

Equipment correlations are on a 1998 Q1 U.S. Gulf Coast basis and are escalated to your dollar-year by CEPCI. A long escalation widens the error band — the estimate class shown on the report reflects that.

The estimate is computed in USD and converted for reporting. Inputs stay in USD.
Leave blank to use the regional default.

Cost index

Every cost in the estimate is moved between dollar-years by the ratio of two CEPCI values, so this table is the one shared number the whole capital estimate rests on. It lists the years this study touches. Type over any of them — a published figure you have and teakit does not, or a projection for a year past the series — and your value is saved with the project and used in place of the shipped one.

03 — Capital

From purchased equipment to total capital

Purchased equipment is typically 15–30% of what a plant costs. This is the NETL ladder: BEC → EPCC → TPC → TOC → TASC.

Installation

How purchased cost becomes installed cost. Items you entered as an installed price skip this step and enter at BEC directly.

2.5–4 is the usual range for a fluid-processing plant. 1.0 would mean no bulk material and no construction labour.

Factor by equipment type

Every kind of machine in the equipment list, with the factor DOE/NETL-2002/1169 gives it — the bulk material and labour for the service that kind of machine sits in, plus the labour to set it. Type over any of them. An item can also carry its own factor, on the item, when one machine is genuinely unlike the rest of its type.

Contingency and EPC

Process contingency covers technology immaturity, project contingency covers estimate immaturity. They are not interchangeable.

NETL uses 15–20%.
AACE 16R-90: 15–30% for a budget estimate.

04 — Operating cost

What the plant spends every year

Depreciation and interest are not operating costs — they are handled by the costing method. Putting them here double-counts capital.

Operating time

Two separate numbers, and every hourly rate is multiplied by both. The hours are how long the plant is available; the capacity factor is how hard it runs while it is. Expressing the same derate in both counts it twice.

Raw materials, catalysts and chemicals

Rates are at full production. A per-year line with the rate set to 1 lets you enter an annual lump sum.

Utilities

Prices default to the EIA industrial series for electricity and gas, and to published engineering estimates for the rest. Replace anything that matters with your own tariff.

Plant-level lines

Consumption that does not belong to one machine — site lighting, HVAC, a flare pilot — or a whole-plant figure you would rather enter as a single line.

Labour

09 — Sensitivity

Which assumptions actually drive the answer?

Usually three or four of them. The rest could be wrong by a factor of two without moving the result.

Parameters

Left blank, teakit picks the discount rate, contingency, capacity factor and your four largest variable-cost lines. Each row shows what the parameter is set to now, and you choose whether its low and high are absolute values or a percentage change from that — the Range column always shows what the study will actually run.
